Your race sets your size, stamina and innate traits. Your class supplies a combat specialty and a starting talent. Practice earns skill levels and talent points. Eight preparation slots hold learned spells, innate abilities, Amulet Step and items carrying Hex programs. Equipment can gain a quality rank through crafting, loot or reforging.

## Melee attacks

With a sword, axe, knife or other supported melee weapon, aim and press attack. Damage is dealt after the wind-up. A sword alternates sweeping cuts and a thrust. Kitchen knives and daggers follow a short jab, a side slash and an overhead strike. An axe has a shorter reach and a narrower swing. Wait between attacks to start the sequence over. The attack range in the weapon's tooltip includes any range bonus from reforging.

One-handed weapons can be paired to alternate hands. A shield still blocks when held up. To change attack controls, open Better Combat in the Mods menu or look for its category under Controls. Holding attack repeats swings by default. You can assign a key to feint and cancel a wind-up.

Wooden and stone swords and axes work at full speed from the start, as does the flint knife. Advanced weapons take 50% longer to recover between swings until you learn the talent shown in the item tooltip. When you fight with two weapons, each swing uses the training for its own weapon. Without Shield Handling, a shield takes five extra ticks to begin blocking. Under **Talents → Classes**, Warrior has heavy weapon and shield training, and Ranger has light weapon training. Equipment keeps its applied enchantments while you train.

## Ranged weapons

The ordinary bow works at full speed from the start. For the crossbow, Starlight Bow and autoloader crossbow, reach Ranged level 2, 6 or 10 respectively, then spend one Ranger point on the named talent under **Talents → Classes → Ranger**. Before training, drawing or reloading takes 45% longer.

## Armor training

Learn Heavy Armor Handling in Warrior and Light Armor Handling in Ranger. The Ranger talent gives ordinary chainmail its full protection. Spell practice earns points for Caster Armor Handling in Mage. Before training, a piece gives a tenth of its usual armor rating and its own bonuses are inactive. Applied enchantments retain their effects. Trained light armor helps you move faster, while heavy armor slows movement by 1% per trained piece or 6% per untrained piece. Leather and other starter clothing are available from the start.

The **Windward** set gives 14 armor points, reduces projectile damage and improves movement while drawing. The full set adds a further 22% arrow damage at eight blocks or farther. The **Duskstride** set gives 13 armor points, speeds up sneaking and sprinting, and cushions melee hits and falls. A charged light-weapon hit that deals damage gives the full set a 28% speed burst for three seconds, with a six-second cooldown. A full iron set gives 15 armor points and trades movement for broader protection. Each specialist set has its own talent in Ranger. A refund leaves worn pieces in place with the undertraining penalty.

The Blue-Footed Boots and Dragonfin Boots keep their native travel abilities independently of armor training.

## Expedition discoveries

Rare study scrolls can turn up in vaults and shared boss loot. Use a scroll in hand to learn its discovery. Your character keeps the discovery after death and can trade any further copies.

**Deep Breath** slows air loss underwater. For **Distraction Throw**, assign the ability in Prepared Abilities, then aim at a block near an idle zombie and activate the assigned slot. The throw spends a snowball. A zombie that can reach the impact may go to investigate and can still notice you.

## Skills and talents

Practice XP appears below Jade's information about the block or creature you are looking at. On the talent tree, a bright green frame marks a talent you can learn now. A checkmark on a dark badge marks a learned talent.

[Project MMO](https://modrinth.com/mod/project-mmo) tracks skills that grow through your own work, from melee and spellcasting to gathering, crafting and handling tools. Each skill reaches level 50, and every level takes a little more practice than the previous one. Death keeps all levels.

Hits with swords, axes and other conventional melee weapons and successful blocks with a shield earn Warrior points through practice. Ranged and light-weapon hits and damage dealt by your Beastmaster companions build the same Ranger balance. Magic practice earns Mage points and credits each native casting pulse with half its actual mana cost after discounts, rounded down with a minimum of one XP. Scroll pulses use their original mana cost and a free recast gives one XP. Continuous spells train while you channel them. Ordinary repeated or safe spell practice keeps giving experience. Enchanting at an enchanting table also trains Magic. Brewing or using potions gives no skill experience.

Mining stone, cutting wood and digging train Gathering. Crafting trains Crafting, while crafting food trains Cooking instead. Taking finished items out of a furnace trains Cooking for food and Smithing for everything else, such as metal or glass; a furnace fed by hoppers trains nobody. Placing construction blocks trains Building. Harvesting ripe crops, breeding animals and making a successful catch train Farming. Ordinary mobs, mob farms where you land the blows and repeated cheap crafts always give some experience.

Open **Talents → Professions**. Mining contains pickaxes, Building contains axes and shovels, and Farming and Animal Husbandry contains hoes and shears. Each family earns its own points from use. Mine rock with a pickaxe, chop or strip wood with an axe, dig or make paths with a shovel, till soil or clear foliage with a hoe, and trim plants or shear animals with shears. Spend a point on the material tier shown in the item's tooltip to unlock full speed for that tier. Wooden and stone tools work at full speed from the start.

Copper and iron tool training are available independently. Steel, diamond and netherite training follow the iron branch. In Mining, you can start with **Sure hand** or choose **Quick work** or **Lucky find** with your first point. Quick work and Lucky find exclude each other.

Before training, a tool breaks suitable blocks more slowly. Some right-click actions, such as tilling or shearing, have a short pause before you can repeat them. A diamond pickaxe can still harvest obsidian. On stone, an iron pickaxe with its talent can work faster than a diamond pickaxe whose talent you have yet to learn. Applied enchantments, including Efficiency and Mending, keep their effects. The Smithing tree still determines which tools you can make.

Warrior, Ranger and Mage each earn and spend their own points. Any character can develop all three trees at the same costs and prerequisites. Subclasses share their class's balance. Warrior contains melee weapons, heavy armor, shields, Defender and Berserker. Ranger groups shooting, light weapons, light armor and animal companions on one tree. Mage keeps the four spell directions on one tree. The [magic guide](../magic/) explains spell learning, scroll ranks and special discoveries.

Magic practice continues earning points at the same rate after level 50. Each profession tree has its own points. Your starting profession funds its selected starter in the matching tree. Smithing earns its first practice point at level 1. Cooking earns its practice points at levels 3 and 10. Skills continue to grow after their point limits.

Magic levels now follow their own practice curve; the other skills keep their previous curves. Mastery readiness and point rewards use the same credited magic XP. For example, Magic level 15 requires 4,301 XP, level 30 requires 21,101, level 45 requires 57,701 and level 50 requires 72,701. Reaching a mastery level opens its prerequisite, while buying the talent still costs a point.

Open the inventory and press **Talents** below the nutrients button. Pick **Classes** or **Professions**, then choose a tree on the second row. A profession's general talents and tool branches sit together. The header names the current balance, including the tool's balance when you select a tool talent. **All Points** shows all three classes, six professions and five tool families together. Zero balances appear dimmed, and a minus sign shows how many points you need to earn in that practice before you can spend again. **Back** restores your selected tree, position and zoom.

Select a node to read its effect and price, then press **Learn**. Drag the tree to move, use the wheel to zoom, or press **Overview** to fit the whole tree. Search highlights matching talents. Enter selects and centers the next match. Some talents exclude each other, such as Onslaught and Firm footing. Dashed links mark these alternatives, and the details name the other choice. Closing Talents or Nutrients returns to the ordinary inventory.

To check your levels, open the inventory and click the **Skills** button with gold bars, above the nutrients button between your character and the crafting grid: a panel attached to the left side of the inventory, where the recipe book also opens, lists combat and profession skills with their progress and a search field; scroll it with the mouse wheel or drag its scrollbar, and press the button again to hide it. The Skills, Nutrients and Talents buttons are also available with the accessory slots open. Skills and accessories replace each other; closing one leaves the other closed. **P** opens the Project MMO glossary in the world. In an inventory, hover over an item and press **Ctrl+P** to inspect it in the glossary. The level list on the game screen is hidden by default so it does not cover the minimap; assign _Toggle Skill List_ in Controls to show it.

## Prepared abilities

Open **Prepared abilities** from the inventory or Curios to assign, clear and rearrange learned spells, racial abilities, Amulet Step and items carrying Hex programs in three wheels with eight slots each, including during combat. Passive talents do not use these slots. Hold the middle mouse button, press 1, 2 or 3 to switch wheels, then release over a slot to select it. Tap the button to use it. A learned spell requires an equipped spellbook focus and keeps its costs and cooldown. Racial abilities and Amulet Step work without a focus. For a Hex program, keep its assigned cypher, trinket or artifact on your hotbar or in your offhand. The [magic guide](../magic/) covers controls, books, scroll study and writing Hex programs. Personal ranks and preparation survive death, relogging and dimension changes.

Learn melee and shield techniques in Warrior and shooting and light-weapon techniques in Ranger. Assign them to the same wheels as other abilities. Activate Shield Brace with a raised shield to hold your ground and reduce shield wear. Counterattack rewards striking the enemy you blocked, and Heavy Strike can interrupt a skeleton or pillager preparing a shot. Use Slowing Shot to create space, Marking Shot to set up a stronger second arrow, or Quick Shot to fire under pressure. Dash covers up to six blocks and opens a stronger light-weapon hit; Disengage covers up to five blocks backwards and softens the next melee hit for a moment. Both stop at obstacles and gaps. Each has its own cooldown, so you can follow one with the other as soon as the first movement ends. Flank Strike rewards hitting from the side or rear.

Select a prepared bow technique in the wheel and press Cast with a regular bow. The bow starts drawing right away and fires automatically at full draw. Keep aiming until release. Switching bows during the draw cancels the shot without spending an arrow or starting its cooldown. Quick Shot reaches full draw sooner.

Learn Triple Shot after Steady Aim in Ranger. Three ordinary inventory arrows follow the release aim in succession, with lighter damage on the final two. Even a bow with Infinity spends all three on this technique.

After learning **Shield Handling** in Warrior, prepare **Shield Shove**. Block a hit, keep the shield in hand and use the technique while the attacker is still in front and within reach. **Wide Shield Shove** also pushes up to three nearby enemies in front. Counterattack and Shield Shove spend the same opening, so choose damage or room to move.

In Melee, **Last Stand** activates when a hit you survive drops you from above 35% health to below it. Land a fully charged melee hit within four seconds for a damage bonus. The passive recovers after 12 seconds. With a heavy weapon, prepare **Offensive Stance** to take 25% more damage for five seconds. Landing a fully charged heavy hit during the stance speeds up one next heavy swing. A miss spends that opening.

In Ranger, learn **Duelist’s Parry** in the light weapon branch and prepare it. Activate it just before a frontal melee hit with a light weapon equipped. A successful parry reduces that hit and briefly speeds up one manual reply. Aim at the attacker and press attack. Arrows and strikes from behind do not open a reply.

Learn **Parry Sidestep** after Duelist’s Parry in Ranger. Hold your left or right movement key when the parry succeeds to take a short step in that direction. The step requires solid ground and stops at obstacles and edges. Use the same parry activation and cooldown. The quicker manual reply is still available if the step is blocked.

### Beastmaster companions

Spend one Ranger point on **Summon Fox**, then add the summons you learn to your preparation wheels. The companion branch progresses from fox to wolf to brown bear. Each talent starts with one animal. A fox upgrade raises the count to three and a wolf upgrade raises it to two. You can summon one bear. Each species has its own damage and protection talents.

A temporary group lasts 60 seconds. A successful summon starts a 150-second cooldown shared by all species and replaces your whole previous group, even when you call the same species. **Faster Summoning** shortens that wait to 120 seconds. If the summon fails, your current group stays. The cooldown keeps running if you lose your companions.

Learn and prepare **Recall Companions** and **Focus Attack** in separate slots. Recall breaks off the chase and calls companions back along a reachable route, even when you aim at an enemy. For Focus Attack, aim at a hostile creature you can see within 24 blocks. Your summons earn Ranger practice for you when they deal qualifying damage, including when they attack on their own.

With **Persistent Companions**, summoned groups have no time limit and living animals follow you on paid waystone trips. They keep their injuries, and the shared summon cooldown keeps running. If you arrive without a safe place for the group, the same animals wait until they can land nearby. Your death dismisses them.

## Making tools and equipment

To make a stone tool, use flint on stone to get knapping rocks. Hold one rock in each hand and use it to start knapping. Shape the tool head, then assemble it with a stick. Making stone tools gives Smithing practice and requires no talent.

The Smithing tree has separate manufacture branches for work tools and combat equipment, starting at iron. Expedition finds can be used as soon as you obtain them. Steel gear can be upgraded with a diamond upgrade template. Find a template and copy it. Learning either **Diamond Work Tools** or **Diamond Arms and Armor** opens **Diamond Template**, which lets you forge a template from materials.

Personal Smithing practice and accurate hammer strikes improve the chances of better initial quality. Automated production grants no personal Smithing practice or workmanship bonus.

### Field repair

Learn **Field Repair** after **Sound Mend** in Smithing. Craft two **Field Repair Kits** from one iron ingot, one leather and one string in any arrangement. Hold a kit in one hand and a damaged tool, weapon or armor piece in the other. Carry a matching anvil repair material, such as an iron ingot for an iron pickaxe. Stand still and hold Use for two seconds.

Success spends one kit and one material and restores up to one eighth of the item's maximum durability. Enchantments and reforging stay on the item. If you move, take damage or interrupt the action before it completes, you keep the kit and material. An anvil can restore more durability per material back at the workshop.

## Equipment quality and reforging

To equip an accessory, hold it and right-click in the air. If it already uses right-click, such as a spyglass, sneak and right-click in the air to put it on. With a single item in hand and no compatible empty slot, you can swap it for a removable equipped accessory. Item tooltips and EMI explain special accessory effects.

Quality ranges from poor to legendary. Excellent gear can specialize in one strong bonus, such as a pickaxe’s mining speed. Epic gear combines two or three bonuses. A legendary roll combines bonuses of that strength with a special effect. For example, a pickaxe can dig a 3×3 face, and a hoe can till a 3×3 bed. Sneak to use these tools on a single block.

Reforging can produce legendary gear. Some legendary gear also appears in selected chests, including swords with **Frost Strike** in dungeons, mineshafts and Ice Spider Dens. A charged hit with that sword has a 20% chance to apply Slowness I for two seconds. Ordinary crafting can roll up to epic quality.

Open the anvil’s **Reforging** tab. Put undamaged gear in the middle, one base material on the left and one lapis lazuli on the right. An iron pickaxe takes an iron ingot, a Wandering Magician Hood takes leather and Artificer’s Cane takes a gold ingot. Each paid attempt commits the whole result immediately. The price and odds are fixed for that item. The new rank can be worse or repeat the previous one. Ordinary enchantments remain on the item.

To compare possible results, open the item in EMI and select **Reforging**. The card shows the materials and the chance of each available rank. Select a rank and hover over a result for its bonuses and any conditions. For example, a good pickaxe can keep its mining-speed bonus and also save wear when mining stone.

Fine and better smithing hammers widen the exact-hit zone on the forging meter. Fine and better tongs can avoid spending durability while protecting you from burns from hot metal in your inventory.

Hex Casting equipment uses charged amethyst or iron.

- **Drawing staves** take charged amethyst. After a mishap, an ordinary staff narrows the error down to four steps. A fine one shows the exact step, and an exceptional one also shows the last pattern that succeeded.
- **Cyphers, trinkets and artifacts** also take charged amethyst. Higher ranks show the remaining charge more precisely. Epic and legendary carriers also show the reason for their last mishap in the tooltip.
- **Jeweler's Hammers** take an iron ingot. Fine quality and above can save durability. Epic and legendary hammers also collect drops from blocks that occupy less than a full cube, such as amethyst clusters. Any drops that do not fit in your inventory stay on the ground.

The autoloader crossbow takes an iron ingot. Its charge bonuses affect automatic loading: click to start, sneak and click to cancel, and fire to start loading the next bolt if you still have ammunition. Reforging keeps an already loaded bolt. Reforged weapons with built-in spells keep their spells and levels.

Ranked spell books and rings provide passive bonuses while equipped in their respective accessory slots. Books keep their quality and reforging bonuses as casting focuses; learned spells belong to the character, and changing books does not grant or remove that knowledge.

Fine and higher grappling hooks reach farther, can avoid wear when they catch a block, and use less hunger when you cast, reel up, or jump off the line. The extra range stacks with Long Reach. Some ranks also favor ascent or descent. A legendary hook can dampen sideways sway while you sneak on an anchored line.

Armor bonuses depend on the slot and situation. A helmet can prepare protection against a second projectile hit, while diving gear can save air or help you push off the bottom. Triggered armor and magic protection share a limit. Some staffs improve eligible paid casts, and a paraglider can hold its course better through a turn.

Field gear has its own results. A brush can retain progress when you pause an excavation. A seed pouch can plant a row from its stored seeds and stops at an obstacle. Reforging preserves the contents of the pouch, the water in a canteen and the film and attachments in a camera.

The legendary handheld rope launcher marks its estimated impact on a solid surface. Crouch in first person with a throwable rope connector in the opposite hand to see the marker.

A pickaxe with **Extra Yield** can add one unit of an actual drop, including stone gathered with Silk Touch. This bonus applies to blocks with tracked natural provenance in newly generated terrain.

Enchanting Mastery III costs one point after Mastery II. The separate efficiency branch reduces the costs of applying enchantments.

[Fresh Animations: Player Extension](https://modrinth.com/resourcepack/fa-player-extension), [Not Enough Animations](https://modrinth.com/project/MPCX6s5C) and [3D Skin Layers](https://modrinth.com/mod/3dskinlayers) change how the character is shown. ## Races and classes

On first joining, preview your race and appearance on your own skin, then choose a class and its starter, a subclass or **Later**, and a profession and its starter. Review your traits, practice bonuses and kit before confirming. A character with a saved race or class starts at the first missing choice. Leaving before confirmation reopens that choice on your next login.

In singleplayer, the world pauses until you confirm. On a server or in a world opened to LAN, others can keep playing. While you choose, attacks and environmental hazards cannot hurt your character, and your status effect timers stay still.

Your race, appearance, class, subclass, profession and starting abilities persist through death, dimension changes and login. Open the portrait button in the inventory or Curios to read the same character sheet again.

Open **Ear color** or **Beard color** during creation or on the character sheet. Use the rainbow strip to pick a hue, then choose its saturation and brightness in the palette. The close-up updates as you drag. Select **Done** during creation or **Apply** on the sheet to keep the choice. **Default color** restores the skin color for ears or the original brown for beards.

### Changing your character

An **Identity Seal** can appear in abandoned mineshaft chest minecarts, desert pyramid chests and shipwreck treasure chests. Each eligible container has a 4% chance to hold one. Hold the seal and choose a new race, class, subclass or starting profession. If you change class, choose its starter and subclass. A new race needs enough space for its body.

The preview shows the new identity, talent refunds and penalties by point pool, changes to starting grants, and affected preparations and equipment. Confirm to consume one seal and apply the identity change and full reset together. Cancelling keeps the seal.

### Redistributing talent points

Craft **Knotted Thread** from two pieces of string, a diamond and an ender pearl in any arrangement. Use it to preview a full reset of your paid talents while keeping your identity. Confirm to spend one thread.

Both items return paid talent points to their original pools with a 10% penalty per pool. For example, of 20 returned points you can spend 18 again. Fractions carry within their pool. Every 3,000 new practice XP in the matching pool restores one lost point. Applicable starting grants still work. A grant alone gives no refund, and an actual purchase of the same talent is refunded once.

Your practice, personal spell ranks and completed scroll study remain. Abilities and worn gear may require you to regain certain talents or mastery. Starter kits and the mage journal are issued only at creation.

## Combat and camera

[Otherworld D&D](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/modpacks/otherworld-dnd) is the reference for expressive animation, meaningful weapon differences and readable hits. Melee, ranged weapons and magic should remain viable styles. The intended equipment can include firearms suited to the setting, ship cannons and explosives alongside swords, bows and crossbows.

Both first and third person should support the main activities. The side-offset third-person camera is already supplied by [Shoulder Surfing](https://modrinth.com/mod/shoulder-surfing-reloaded), independently of the planned combat changes; [graphics and animation](../quality-of-life/presentation/#character-and-camera) describes its controls.

Danger should depend mainly on the place: its creatures, biome, dungeon, height and dimension. Moderate growth of threats over time is also desired; the rules remain to be chosen.
