Your race sets your size, stamina and innate traits. Your class supplies a combat specialty and a starting talent. Practice earns skill levels and talent points. Eight preparation slots hold learned spells, innate abilities, Amulet Step and items carrying Hex programs. Equipment can gain a quality rank through crafting, loot or reforging.

## Skills and talents

[Project MMO](https://modrinth.com/mod/project-mmo) tracks nine skills that grow through your own work: Melee, Ranged and Magic for combat, and Gathering, Crafting, Smithing, Building, Farming and Cooking for peaceful activities. Each skill reaches level 50, and every level takes a little more practice than the previous one. Death keeps all levels. Skills never forbid using an item, block or tool.

Hits with melee weapons train Melee and projectiles train Ranged. Magic practice credits each native casting pulse with half its actual mana cost after discounts, rounded down with a minimum of one XP. Scroll pulses use their original mana cost even though the scroll is consumed instead of mana; a free recast gives one XP. Continuous spells train while you channel them. Ordinary repeated or safe practice stays positive, without fatigue, daily limits or source caps.

Mining stone, cutting wood and digging train Gathering. Crafting trains Crafting, while crafting food trains Cooking instead. Taking finished items out of a furnace trains Cooking for food and Smithing for everything else, such as metal or glass; a furnace fed by hoppers trains nobody. Placing blocks trains Building, and growing crops, breeding animals and fishing train Farming. Ordinary mobs, mob farms where you land the blows and repeated cheap crafts always give some experience.

Melee, Ranged and Magic fill one shared **combat budget**, capped at 142 points for the current catalogue. Melee and Ranged still give one point per five skill levels. Magic gives one starting point, then earns points from accumulated practice independently of its level milestones:

| Credited magic XP | Interval between points | Total magic-earned points at the end of the stage |
| ----------------- | ----------------------: | ------------------------------------------------: |
| 0 to 500          |                  100 XP |                   6, including the starting point |
| 500 to 5,000      |                  500 XP |                                                15 |
| 5,000 to 20,000   |                1,500 XP |                                                25 |
| Above 20,000      |                3,000 XP |                                 141 at 368,000 XP |

Magic practice continues earning points at the same rate after level 50. With the one point paid by your class, you can fund 131 magic talents and 11 compatible physical talents. The separate profession pool has ten points. Your profession pays for its starting talent, and every five levels in a peaceful skill earn another point up to that cap. Ten points cover all eight professional talents, the gathering root and either mining speed or fortune. Skills continue to grow after the cap.

Magic levels now follow their own practice curve; the other skills keep their previous curves. Mastery readiness and point rewards use the same credited magic XP. For example, Magic level 15 requires 4,301 XP, level 30 requires 21,101, level 45 requires 57,701 and level 50 requires 72,701. Reaching a mastery level opens its prerequisite, while buying the talent still costs a point.

Open the inventory and press **Talents**, below the nutrients button. Combat, Magic and Professions have separate named pages; spending a point on Magic also reduces the points available on Combat. Select a node to read its effect and price, then press **Learn**. Drag the tree to move, use the wheel to zoom, or press **Center** to fit the whole page. Search highlights matching talents; Enter selects and centers the next match. Some talents exclude each other, such as Onslaught and Firm footing. Dashed links mark these alternatives, and the details name the other choice. Every character can open every branch. Closing Talents or Nutrients returns to the ordinary inventory.

To check your levels, open the inventory and click the **Skills** button with gold bars, above the nutrients button between your character and the crafting grid: a panel attached to the left side of the inventory, where the recipe book also opens, lists combat and profession skills with their progress and a search field; scroll it with the mouse wheel or drag its scrollbar, and press the button again to hide it. The Skills, Nutrients and Talents buttons are also available with the accessory slots open. Skills and accessories replace each other; closing one leaves the other closed. **P** opens the Project MMO glossary for the player, the block or the creature you look at. The level list on the game screen is hidden by default so it does not cover the minimap; assign _Toggle Skill List_ in Controls to show it.

Combat offers melee, archery and protection talents. Open Professions for the eight job talents and the gathering branch. Magic is one tree with a common introductory repertoire and four functional directions covering all 111 spells. Its mastery gates depend on Magic practice, while ordinary bundles, individual spell unlocks and useful passive branches share 131 one-point nodes. The [magic guide](../magic/) explains learning, scroll ranks and special discoveries.

## Prepared abilities

Open **Prepared abilities** from the inventory or Curios to assign, clear and rearrange learned spells, racial abilities, Amulet Step and items carrying Hex programs in eight shared slots, including during combat. Passive talents do not use these slots. Hold the middle mouse button and release over a slot in the wheel to select it. Tap the button to use it. A learned spell requires an equipped spellbook focus and keeps its costs and cooldown. Racial abilities and Amulet Step work without a focus. For a Hex program, keep its assigned cypher, trinket or artifact on your hotbar or in your offhand. The [magic guide](../magic/) covers controls, books, scroll study and writing Hex programs. Personal ranks and preparation survive death, relogging and dimension changes.

## Equipment quality and reforging

To equip an accessory, hold it and right-click in the air. If it already uses right-click, such as a spyglass, sneak and right-click in the air to put it on. With a single item in hand and no compatible empty slot, you can swap it for a removable equipped accessory. Item tooltips and EMI explain special accessory effects.

Quality ranges from poor to legendary. Excellent gear can specialize in one strong bonus, such as a pickaxe’s mining speed. Epic gear combines two or three bonuses. A legendary roll combines bonuses of that strength with a special effect. For example, a pickaxe can dig a 3×3 face, and a hoe can till a 3×3 bed. Sneak to use these tools on a single block.

Reforging can produce legendary gear. Some legendary gear also appears in selected chests, including swords with **Frost Strike** in dungeons, mineshafts and Ice Spider Dens. A charged hit with that sword has a 20% chance to apply Slowness I for two seconds. Ordinary crafting can roll up to epic quality.

Open the anvil’s **Reforging** tab. Put undamaged gear in the middle, one base material on the left and one lapis lazuli on the right. An iron pickaxe takes an iron ingot, a Wandering Magician Hood takes leather and Artificer’s Cane takes a gold ingot. Each paid attempt commits the whole result immediately. The price and odds are fixed for that item. The new rank can be worse or repeat the previous one. Ordinary enchantments remain on the item.

To compare possible results, open the item in EMI and select **Reforging**. The card shows the materials and the chance of each available rank. Select a rank and hover over a result for its bonuses and any conditions. For example, a good pickaxe can keep its mining-speed bonus and also save wear when mining stone.

Hex Casting equipment uses charged amethyst or iron.

- **Drawing staves** take charged amethyst. After a mishap, an ordinary staff narrows the error down to four steps. A fine one shows the exact step, and an exceptional one also shows the last pattern that succeeded.
- **Cyphers, trinkets and artifacts** also take charged amethyst. Higher ranks show the remaining charge more precisely. Epic and legendary carriers also show the reason for their last mishap in the tooltip.
- **Jeweler's Hammers** take an iron ingot. Fine quality and above can save durability. Epic and legendary hammers also collect drops from blocks that occupy less than a full cube, such as amethyst clusters. Any drops that do not fit in your inventory stay on the ground.

The autoloader crossbow takes an iron ingot. Its charge bonuses affect automatic loading: click to start, sneak and click to cancel, and fire to start loading the next bolt if you still have ammunition. Reforging keeps an already loaded bolt. Reforged weapons with built-in spells keep their spells and levels.

Ranked spell books and rings provide passive bonuses while equipped in their respective accessory slots. Books keep their quality and reforging bonuses as casting focuses; learned spells belong to the character, and changing books does not grant or remove that knowledge.

Fine and higher grappling hooks reach farther, can avoid wear when they catch a block, and use less hunger when you cast, reel up, or jump off the line. The extra range stacks with Long Reach. Some ranks also favor ascent or descent. A legendary hook can dampen sideways sway while you sneak on an anchored line.

Armor bonuses depend on the slot and situation. A helmet can prepare protection against a second projectile hit, while diving gear can save air or help you push off the bottom. Triggered armor and magic protection share a limit. Some staffs improve eligible paid casts, and a paraglider can hold its course better through a turn.

Field gear has its own results. A brush can retain progress when you pause an excavation. A seed pouch can plant a row from its stored seeds and stops at an obstacle. Reforging preserves the contents of the pouch, the water in a canteen and the film and attachments in a camera.

The legendary handheld rope launcher marks its estimated impact on a solid surface. Crouch in first person with a throwable rope connector in the opposite hand to see the marker.

A pickaxe with **Extra Yield** can add one unit of an actual drop, including stone gathered with Silk Touch. This bonus applies to blocks with tracked natural provenance in newly generated terrain.

## Races and classes

On first joining, preview your race and appearance on your own skin, then choose a class, a profession and a starting ability from each. Review your traits, practice bonuses and kit before confirming. A character with a saved race or class starts at the first missing choice. Leaving before confirmation reopens that choice on your next login.

In singleplayer, the world pauses until you confirm. On a server or in a world opened to LAN, others can keep playing. While you choose, attacks and environmental hazards cannot hurt your character, and your status effect timers stay still.

Your race, appearance, class, profession and both starting abilities persist through death, dimension changes and login. Open the portrait button in the inventory or Curios to read the same character sheet again.

| Race   | Hearts | Stamina | In play                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------ | -----: | ------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Human  |     10 |   1,000 | Standard size, movement and learning. Keep your original skin or add tied hair or a short beard.                                                                          |
| Dwarf  |     12 |   1,100 | Shorter and broader, with greater knockback resistance. Walks 5% slower and has one extra point of cold resistance. Choose a bare face, a forked beard or a banded beard. |
| Goblin |      8 |     850 | Fits under a one-block ceiling when crouched. Sneaks faster and retains ordinary reach and one-block jumps. Choose wide, notched or drooping ears.                        |
| Elf    |      9 |     900 | Tall and lean. Moves 6% faster and gains 20% more practice from archery and magic. Choose swept, upright or leaf-shaped ears.                                             |

Dwarves can prepare **Dwarven night vision** and activate it again to switch it off. Elves can prepare **Elven light** for a minute of light that travels with them. The light helps you see and leaves the world's light levels intact, so use torches for spawn protection. Both abilities work without a spellbook. Appearance choices within a race share one set of stats and dimensions.

### Starting classes

| Class   | Permanent trait                               | Starting ability                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Warrior | +2 armor                                      | Raise a shield in 3 ticks instead of 5, or walk 25% faster while holding it up.                                                                                                         |
| Ranger  | Fully drawn bow shots travel 10% faster       | Walk 25% faster while drawing a bow, or halve arrow spread while standing still on the ground.                                                                                          |
| Mage    | +20 maximum mana                              | First magic mastery grants eight rank-1 spells and opens the four magic paths. The Flimsy Journal is your starting focus. Equip it and prepare the spells you want in your eight slots. |
| Rogue   | +5% ground speed while holding a light weapon | Recover light-weapon attacks 10% faster, or deal 15% more damage with a fully charged opening hit against a target at full health.                                                      |

Warriors and rogues learn melee 20% faster, rangers learn archery 20% faster, and mages learn magic 20% faster. Elven archery and magic bonuses add to the class bonus. An elf ranger earns 14 archery XP for an action worth 10 before bonuses. Your profession separately accelerates its peaceful discipline.

The class pays for the chosen starting talent, leaving your ordinary starting point available. Other characters can learn the same talent through the combat or magic tree. Light weapons include the five Farmer's Delight knives, the Wetland Whimsy dagger and the Iron's Spells 'n Spellbooks amethyst rapier.

### Starting professions

Choose a profession independently of your race and class. It gives 20% more practice XP in its own discipline, a small kit and one of two talents. These talents work passively. Any character can learn the other talents later on the Professions page.

| Profession | Choose one starting talent                                                                                                             | Your kit                                         |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Blacksmith | Restore 25% more durability per repair material on the anvil, or pay one fewer XP level for material repairs (minimum one).            | Stone pickaxe, 4 coal                            |
| Builder    | Reach one block farther to place, use or break blocks, or dismantle construction blocks 15% faster with the appropriate tool.          | Stone axe, 16 cobblestone                        |
| Farmer     | Gain a 10% chance of one extra unit of ripe produce when harvesting by hand, or a 25% chance to spare your hoe when tilling.           | Wooden hoe, 6 wheat seeds                        |
| Cook       | Meals you prepare give one extra saturation point, or take 25% less time to eat. The benefit travels with the food to whoever eats it. | 2 bowls, a red and a brown mushroom, flint knife |

Your starter kit is issued once. If your inventory is full, make room to receive the remaining items. A machine working unattended earns no personal bonuses.

### Changing your character

An **Identity Seal** can appear in abandoned mineshaft chest minecarts, desert pyramid chests and shipwreck treasure chests. Each eligible container has a 4% chance to hold one. Use the seal in your hand, choose one change and review it before confirming. Cancelling keeps the seal.

- **Race.** Choose a different race and preview its appearance on your skin. Stand somewhere with room for the new body.
- **Class.** Pick the new class and starter. Keep your learned talents or refund the old class branch in full. For a mage this branch includes the magic tree. If keeping your investment leaves no free point for the new starter, the starter works immediately and uses the next combat point you earn.
- **Profession.** Pick the new profession and starter. Talents belonging to the old profession return their points to the profession pool. Common gathering talents and other professions’ talents stay learned.

You keep your practice XP, personal spell ranks, eight preparation slots and current cooldowns. Assigned abilities whose requirements you lose become unavailable. Starter kits and the mage journal are issued only at creation.

### Redistributing talent points

Craft **Knotted Thread** from two pieces of string, a diamond and an ender pearl in any arrangement. Use it, open the combat, magic or profession page and mark learned talents. Review the list before spending the thread. It includes any additional talents that would lose their prerequisites. Your current starting abilities stay learned.

Combat and magic share one pool. Professions have their own. Returning 20 points to one pool frees 18 for spending. Smaller returns carry tenths between uses in the same pool, so ten separate one-point returns cost one point in total. Each 3,000 new practice XP in that pool restores one lost point, even after reaching the normal point cap.

### Planned subclasses

Subclasses are planned later, before `1.0.0`. The first roster is defender and berserker for warriors, marksman and beastmaster for rangers, elementalist and necromancer for mages, and duelist and poisoner for rogues. Every character can develop any specialization, including advanced talents, with the same prerequisites and point costs regardless of starting class. Their benefits should work together, with active techniques that feel expressive while remaining within reasonable limits.

Future subclasses share the combat budget with magic and classes.

Martial techniques are planned for the same eight preparation slots. Saved sets that players can edit and switch between remain under consideration.

## Combat and camera

[Otherworld D&D](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/modpacks/otherworld-dnd) is the reference for expressive animation, meaningful weapon differences and readable hits. Melee, ranged weapons and magic should remain viable styles. The intended equipment can include firearms suited to the setting, ship cannons and explosives alongside swords, bows and crossbows.

Both first and third person should support the main activities. The side-offset third-person camera is already supplied by [Shoulder Surfing](https://modrinth.com/mod/shoulder-surfing-reloaded), independently of the planned combat changes; [graphics and animation](../quality-of-life/presentation/#character-and-camera) describes its controls.

Danger should depend mainly on the place: its creatures, biome, dungeon, height and dimension. Moderate growth of threats over time is also desired; the rules remain to be chosen.
