---
title: A manifesto on AI and authorship
description: Why I support AI in creative work and oppose excluding primarily AI-generated projects.
status: available
---

I consider artificial intelligence a legitimate tool for creative work and development. Its use alone proves nothing about the quality of a result, an author's good faith or the effort involved. I want Aeroscapes Reimagine to be judged by its design, how it plays and how responsibly it is developed.

Modrinth's policy on generative AI prompted this manifesto. I consider excluding projects based on their predominant method of creation a Luddite approach: it denies an author access to the public catalogue regardless of the quality of the particular result. I also understand the concerns of people who do not want to search for a useful mod among countless hastily generated, untested projects.

## What Modrinth actually says

[Section 6 of the Content Rules](https://modrinth.com/legal/rules/) prohibits publicly publishing projects whose contents are entirely or primarily created or derived from generative AI output. Disclosure is required for substantial generated code, any primarily generated assets, design or functionality that relies on generative AI, and AI use in page text or publishing. Images generated or edited with generative AI are prohibited on project pages, including icons and galleries.

In its [August 13, 2026 announcement](https://modrinth.com/news/article/ai-policy-and-disclosures/), Modrinth allows projects with a primary and significant human contribution. It also states that generating an entire work with AI does not make it the author's work: “generating it completely with AI is not your work”. Disclosures and persistent search exclusion filters already exist; harassment of authors over those disclosures is explicitly prohibited.

The [August 24 explanation](https://support.modrinth.com/en/articles/16551575-disclosure-and-usage-of-ai) calls quality a secondary motivation, welcomes subjectively bad projects and cites ethical, economic and ecological concerns. The platform also fears rapid content production with an extractive attitude towards the community, even when the result is subjectively good. Heavy AI use in one part may be permitted alongside significant human content elsewhere. Primarily generated projects can, in most cases, be unlisted: accessible by link but absent from public search.

My disagreement therefore concerns restricted public visibility and the view of authorship behind it. The term AI-first is not itself a criterion in these rules.

## Why I disagree with this boundary

I understand a platform's wish to sustain a community of creators and protect players' attention. A flood of repetitive projects creates a real problem for discovery and moderation. But a project's method of creation does not establish its usefulness, design quality or reliability. Those properties need checking in any mod or modpack.

Since Modrinth explicitly accepts imperfect human work and does not identify quality as the main reason for the prohibition, the disagreement concerns which work the platform recognises as authorship. I disagree with that choice. I consider defining a problem, designing a solution, selecting alternatives, testing ideas and taking responsibility for the result meaningful parts of authorship. The amount of code or text typed manually does not capture that contribution.

This matters particularly to me in modpacks. Selecting compatible systems, understanding their interactions, shaping a gameplay experience and deciding what belongs in it are work in their own right. They remain work when a tool helps write a configuration, investigate a problem or build a prototype.

I consider the ethical, economic and ecological objections to generative AI cited by Modrinth unfounded. To me, using this tool does not in itself make a project harmful or unacceptable. A detailed debate on these topics is beyond the scope of this manifesto, which concerns authorship and the opportunity to publish.

## Vision, organisation and authorship

I see a leftist, Marxist bias in the claim that a fully generated result is not its author's work. I reject a criterion under which vision, organisation, decisive choices and the risk undertaken can be insufficient to recognise work as one's own simply because execution was entrusted to a tool. I consider those contributions parts of creating a product in their own right.

A company's founder may never operate a machine, yet organise production, invest funds, choose a strategy and bear the risk of failure. The work of engineers and production workers retains its own value. A founder's lack of hands-on manufacturing does not establish that they had no part in bringing the product into existence. For me, this example shows the importance of organisation and responsibility without making the owner the personal author of every component.

In art, separating design from execution also has a history. [The Uffizi describes](https://www.uffizi.it/en/artworks/verrocchio-leonardo-baptism-of-christ) a practice common in fifteenth-century studios: the studio head designed a work, assigning secondary parts to pupils and collaborators. The museum attributes The Baptism of Christ to Andrea del Verrocchio and his pupil Leonardo da Vinci. The pupil's contribution is recognised alongside the master's; the involvement of several people does not deprive a work of authorship.

These examples help me explain the value of contributions beyond direct execution. For me, using AI raises the same question about vision, selection and direction, although a tool and an independent co-author have different roles in the process. I want a person's role to be assessed through their actual decisions and contribution to the result. The share of operations performed by a tool does not describe that on its own.

## What AI offers an author

For me, AI's value lies in exploring ideas faster, examining code and configurations, finding mistakes and testing small proofs of concept before investing in a full implementation. Additional review helps reveal edge cases and interactions that are easy to miss when working alone. A model's response remains a proposal that needs verification.

An independent author may lack a large team, regular testers or a community ready to investigate every issue on GitHub. AI can help with tasks that would otherwise exceed the time or assistance available. It can make starting a project possible at all.

Someone can have a clear understanding of the world they want to create and how it should play without being proficient in programming, design, translation and every development tool at once. I do not consider a missing skill sufficient reason to deny them the opportunity to realise that vision through automation.

This does not mean every AI-assisted task takes more effort than doing it manually, or that a model can replace any specialist. Effort depends on the task. In a carefully developed project, it goes into requirements, context, comparing alternatives, integration, testing and corrections. Knowing that AI was used does not establish that the author did no work.

I also struggle to understand why using templates, generators and other forms of automation is acceptable in itself, while generative AI becomes grounds for rejecting an entire project. Different tools can carry different risks. I want those differences and the consequences of using them to be discussed concretely.

## The platform approach I support

I support honest AI disclosure and the ability for users to exclude that content from their search results. Modrinth already provides this. My proposal is to admit primarily generated projects to the public catalogue too, with clear disclosures and the same general content requirements.

A platform can offer convenient filters, separate collections, recommendation preferences and ways to leave useful feedback. Where necessary, it can limit bulk submissions and reduce the visibility of repetitive or poor content using understandable criteria. Authors should have an opportunity to show their work, and players should be able to choose what they see and install.

Popularity, downloads and feedback help people choose, but do not guarantee safety or replace verification. Platforms need to address spam, deception, rights violations and unverified promises regardless of the tool involved. I want neither a catalogue where useful projects drown in junk nor a ban that excludes conscientious authors in advance because of their working method.

People have a right to avoid AI and content made with it. I favour a system where they can express that preference through a setting. Dismissing other people's work and harassing authors adds nothing to that choice.

## What this means for Aeroscapes Reimagine

I dropped the AI-first label to preserve the possibility of publishing on Modrinth. The current project description places direction and acceptance of changes with me; AI helps explore ideas, research modding and review code and pack integration. This is a practical distribution decision, and it does not mean I agree with the restriction. A label alone does not establish compliance: the actual contents and honest disclosure of AI use matter.

I am responsible for the decisions I adopt, verifying the result and correcting mistakes. AI assistance gives the pack no exemption from quality expectations and does not diminish the work of the authors of its included mods, assets and source projects. Their contributions and terms are recorded in the [credits and sources](../credits/).

Both language versions of this manifesto were prepared with AI assistance from the position I set out.
